# Health, Safety, and Vaccination Protocols for a Boarding & Daycare Facility

Operational health and safety reference for a dog boarding, daycare, and grooming facility (Grayslake, IL). This is the medical/biosecurity companion to the operations best-practices doc — it goes deeper on vaccines, disease containment, emergency response, and disinfection. US/Illinois-aware. **Not veterinary advice** — final protocols must be reviewed and signed off by a licensed veterinarian and reconciled to current Illinois law and Lake County animal-control rules.

---

## 1. Vaccination Policy

A facility vaccine policy has two jobs: protect the population, and create a defensible paper trail. Require **proof from the issuing veterinary clinic** (clinic-stamped certificate or vet-portal record) — never accept a verbal claim or an owner's handwritten note. Store the proof and the expiration date in the client record and **block check-in on any expired core vaccine**.

### Required (non-negotiable for entry)

| Vaccine | Protects against | Typical validity | Facility timing rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Rabies** | Rabies | 1-yr or 3-yr product (per the vet's choice) | Required by IL law for dogs ≥4 months. Must be current on file. |
| **DHPP / DAPP** | Distemper, (Hepatitis/Adenovirus), Parvovirus, Parainfluenza | 1-yr or 3-yr depending on product/schedule | Must be current. Puppies must have completed their series. |
| **Bordetella** (kennel cough) | *Bordetella bronchiseptica* | Labeled 12 months; **immunity often wanes by ~6 months** | Require within the last **12 months**, and at least **48 hours before check-in** so it can take effect. Many high-density facilities require it every **6 months** — recommended here. |

### Recommended (strongly encourage; consider requiring)

| Vaccine | Notes |
|---|---|
| **Canine Influenza (CIV) — bivalent H3N2 + H3N8** | Not legally required, but the AAHA 2022 guidelines recommend it for dogs that board, attend daycare, are shown, train in groups, or visit dog parks — i.e. exactly this population. **Critical timing:** a dog with no CIV history needs **two doses, 2–4 weeks apart**, and is **not protected until ~14 days after the second dose**. A booster follows one year later. Tell owners to start ~6 weeks before a first stay. A "get it next week before boarding" request is too late. |
| **Leptospirosis** | Often bundled into DHLPP. Reasonable to recommend given outdoor play yards, standing water, and wildlife exposure in northern IL. |

**Rabies note:** Illinois requires rabies vaccination for dogs. Keep the facility policy aligned to current IL Department of Public Health and Lake County requirements rather than a generic national default; verify before finalizing.

**Puppy exception path:** decide and document whether you accept under-vaccinated puppies (e.g., daycare for socialization before the full series completes). The conservative, lower-liability default is to require the **completed core series + rabies** before group play.

## 2. Kennel Cough / CIRDC — Outbreak Containment & Isolation

"Kennel cough" is really **CIRDC** (Canine Infectious Respiratory Disease Complex) — a cluster of pathogens (*Bordetella*, parainfluenza, canine influenza, adenovirus, *Mycoplasma*, and others). Vaccination reduces but does **not eliminate** risk, because no single vaccine covers every pathogen in the complex. Plan for cases, not just prevention.

### The three levers of prevention
1. **Vaccination** (Bordetella + CIV + DHPP as above).
2. **Stress reduction** — shorter length of stay, less crowding, quiet rest periods. Stress suppresses immunity and is a major driver of outbreaks.
3. **Sanitation + ventilation** — most CIRDC pathogens survive only hours to a few weeks in the environment and are killed by routine disinfectants, so cleaning and air turnover are highly effective.

### Recognize it early (front-line staff checklist)
Watch for: honking/hacking cough, retching after coughing, nasal or eye discharge, sneezing, lethargy, reduced appetite, fever. **Any dog showing signs is pulled from group play immediately.**

### Isolation protocol
- **Isolate at first sign** — move the dog to a dedicated isolation room/run physically separated from the general population, ideally on a **separate air handling zone** (do not share recirculated air with healthy dogs).
- **Dedicated equipment** per isolation: own bowls, leash, cleaning tools, color-coded so they never go back to the main floor.
- **Staff sequencing:** care for healthy dogs first, isolated/sick dogs **last**. Change/cover clothing, and wash hands / change gloves between zones. No moving from sick to healthy without full hygiene.
- **Duration:** keep isolated and out of group play until cleared. A practical floor is **at least 14 days for serious cases** (and longer — until well past symptom resolution — for pneumonia), but defer to the treating vet's clearance for the specific pathogen. For mild uncomplicated cough, send home and require a symptom-free interval before return.
- **Notify the owner** to pick up / seek vet care, and **notify the vet** of a possible cluster.

### Outbreak response (multiple cases)
1. **Pause new intake** into affected groups; consider a short admissions hold.
2. **Contact-trace** — identify dogs that shared space/time with the index case; notify those owners to watch for signs.
3. **Deep-clean and increase ventilation/air exchanges** in affected areas.
4. **Log everything** — dates, dogs, symptoms, actions — for the vet and for liability.
5. Review whether to **tighten the CIV/Bordetella requirement** (e.g., move Bordetella to every 6 months) after an event.

## 3. Emergency Veterinary Plan

Every facility must have a **written, posted, vet-reviewed emergency plan** that any staff member can execute under stress.

- **Primary vet on record** — a named local clinic that knows the facility, with a written program of veterinary care (disease control, vaccination, routine + emergency care).
- **After-hours / 24-hour emergency hospital** — name, address, phone posted at every staff station and pre-programmed in the facility phone. Identify the nearest 24/7 emergency vet to the Grayslake location and keep a backup.
- **Transport plan** — who drives, which vehicle, who stays to cover the floor. Never leave the remaining population unsupervised.
- **Owner authorization on file** — every intake form must capture: emergency contact(s), a **second** contact, the owner's regular vet, and a **signed treatment-authorization with a dollar cap** ("authorize up to $___ in emergency care if we can't reach you"). This removes the freeze-up moment.
- **First-aid kit + staff trained on it** — bleeding control, cooling for heat emergencies, choke response, basic wound care, and **pet CPR**.
- **Decision rule for staff:** when in doubt, cool/stabilize and transport — do not wait to reach the owner first. Stabilizing red-flag signs (collapse, seizures, can't stand, labored breathing, repeated vomiting, bloody diarrhea, confusion, bloat/distended abdomen) = go now.
- **Disaster/continuity plan** — written plan for fire, severe weather, power loss, or evacuation, including where animals go and how owners are notified. (Often a licensing requirement.)

## 4. Feeding, Medication & Recordkeeping

### Feeding
- Capture each dog's **food, amount, frequency, and special instructions** at intake. Default to owner-supplied food to avoid GI upset; document any house-food substitution.
- **Log every meal served** (and whether the dog ate). Appetite is an early illness signal — a skipped meal should trigger a wellness check.
- Fresh water available at all times; bowls checked and refilled on every rotation (more often in heat — see §6).

### Medication administration
- Record at intake: **drug name, dose, route, time(s), and reason**, plus the owner's written instruction.
- **Only trained staff administer meds.** Use a med log with a **double-check / initials** entry for every dose: dog, drug, dose, time, given-by.
- Store medications securely and separately per dog (labeled), refrigerated where required.
- Flag high-risk meds (insulin, seizure meds, anything time-critical) so they're never missed on a shift handoff.

### Records to keep per stay
- Vaccination proof + expirations
- Feeding log
- Medication log
- Behavior/wellness notes (energy, stool, appetite, mood)
- Incident reports (see §7)
- Emergency authorization + contacts

A boarding/daycare software system (the kennel app being built) should be the single source of truth for all of the above so nothing lives only on paper.

## 5. Parasite / Flea & Tick Policy

- **Require year-round flea/tick prevention** (and ideally heartworm prevention) as a condition of stay, or at minimum **inspect at intake** and refuse/quarantine any dog with live fleas to protect the population.
- **Visible fleas at intake** → do not admit to group space; isolate, notify owner, treat-or-send-home per policy. One untreated dog can seed the whole facility.
- Northern Illinois has real **tick exposure** (Lyme, etc.) from grassy/wooded play areas — do tick checks after outdoor time in season.
- Keep a written protocol for an in-facility flea finding: isolate the dog, launder bedding hot, vacuum and treat the environment, and monitor.

## 6. Heat & Cold Safety

Dogs cool themselves mainly by panting and are far less efficient than people — brachycephalic (flat-faced), overweight, senior, and dark-coated dogs overheat fastest.

### Heat (the bigger risk)
**Stages:** heat *stress* (heavy panting, thirst, still acting normal) → heat *exhaustion* (body temp >103°F) → **heatstroke** (>105°F, life-threatening).

**Prevention:**
- Use **air-conditioned indoor play rooms** in hot weather; limit outdoor time to what's necessary.
- Limit hard exercise during the hottest part of the day; offer shade and cool-water kiddie pools, refreshed each rotation.
- **Check and refill water more frequently** in heat; carry water on floor walk-throughs.
- Never leave a dog in a hot vehicle or unventilated space.

**Emergency cooling:** move to shade/AC, apply **cool (not ice-cold) tap water** to the body plus airflow/fan for rapid heat removal, offer water if conscious, and transport to the vet. **Do not drape wet towels over the dog** — they trap heat. Cool first, then go.

### Cold (real in IL winters)
- Limit outdoor exposure in freezing temps; short potty breaks, not long play, in extreme cold.
- Watch small, thin-coated, young, and senior dogs for shivering/lethargy (hypothermia signs); bring them in.
- Keep indoor temperatures comfortable; provide raised, dry bedding away from drafts.
- Watch for ice-melt/salt on paws (irritant/toxic if licked) on any outdoor surfaces.

Set written **temperature thresholds** (e.g., shorten/cancel outdoor play above/below set °F) so staff aren't guessing.

## 7. Cleaning & Disinfection (Parvo-Grade Cadence)

Routine cleaning controls CIRDC; **parvo and other resistant pathogens demand a higher standard.** The killing power comes from the right product **at the right dilution left wet for the full contact time** — wiping it on and off immediately does nothing.

### Product standards
- **Bleach (sodium hypochlorite):** parvo-effective on non-porous surfaces at **~1:32 (≈½ cup per gallon)** with a **10-minute wet contact time**, then rinse. Harsh; ventilate; never mix with ammonia/other cleaners.
- **Accelerated Hydrogen Peroxide (AHP, e.g., Rescue/Accel):** daily use ~**1:64, 5 min**; terminal/between-occupant clean and parvo-level use ~**1:32, 10 min**. Safer for staff and surfaces. *Caveat:* some independent studies question AHP reliability against parvovirus specifically — for a confirmed parvo exposure, **bleach is the safer call** on non-porous surfaces. Verify the specific product's EPA label claim for canine parvovirus.
- **Potassium peroxymonosulfate (e.g., Trifectant/Virkon):** parvo-capable; ~10-minute saturation; usable on some kennel/yard surfaces.
- Always confirm **EPA-registered, label claim for the target pathogen**, and follow the label dilution/contact time exactly.

### Cadence
- **Daily:** sanitize and disinfect bowls, toys, play surfaces, high-touch areas, and any shared equipment. Spot-clean accidents immediately.
- **Between occupants (run/kennel turnover):** full terminal clean — remove organic matter (pre-clean: soap + scrub, because feces/dirt inactivate disinfectant), then disinfect at the higher concentration/contact time.
- **After any infectious case (parvo/CIRDC/GI):** parvo-grade protocol on the run **and** everything the dog touched; isolate the run from rotation until done; dedicated tools for that run.
- **Pre-clean before you disinfect — always.** Disinfectants don't work through dirt and feces.

### Porous surfaces
Parvovirus is extremely hardy and survives a long time in soil/grass/porous materials. **Carpet, grass yards, and unsealed wood can't be reliably disinfected** — favor sealed, non-porous, easily-cleaned flooring; rotate/rest contaminated yard areas; replace porous items after a parvo case.

## 8. Incident Documentation

Maintain an **incident file** (digital, in the kennel app) for any: injury or illness needing vet care, dog fight/bite, animal death, escape, or near-miss.

Each incident report should capture:
- Date, time, location, dog(s) and owner(s) involved
- Staff present / witnessing
- What happened (factual, no speculation)
- Action taken (first aid, vet contacted/visited, owner notified, when)
- Outcome and follow-up
- Staff signature

Why it matters: continuity of care, pattern detection (a dog or play group that keeps generating incidents), owner transparency, **and liability defense.** Notify the owner of any incident the same day, and document that you did.
