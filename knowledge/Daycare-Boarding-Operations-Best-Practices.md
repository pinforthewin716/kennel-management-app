# Dog Daycare & Boarding Operational Best Practices (US)

Practical operating standards for a US dog daycare/boarding facility. **Note:** there is **no single federal standard** governing staff ratios, capacity, or vaccination policy — the closest industry benchmark is the **International Boarding & Pet Services Association (IBPSA)**. State and local ordinances vary, so always check your state's animal-facility licensing rules in addition to the guidance below.

## Staff-to-dog ratios

- **Active supervised group play: 1 staff per 10–15 dogs** (IBPSA benchmark). Most well-run facilities operate **closer to 1:10** during active play.
- **Always a minimum of 2 staff** present for any active play group (so one can intervene/render aid while supervision continues).
- **Move toward 1:8–1:10 (more staff)** when: dogs are new/unassessed, reactive, high-energy breed mixes, large groups that exceed a person's field of view, or mixed-size groups (large dogs with small/timid dogs).
- **Move toward 1:15 (fewer staff)** only with: established/compatible groups, calm temperaments, experienced trained staff, and a layout with clear sightlines.
- Density matters as much as headcount: 15 dogs in 5,000 sq ft is very different from 15 dogs in 800 sq ft. Schedule staff by **activity level, not just headcount** — it lowers incidents and staff burnout.

## Capacity & grouping

- **Separate dogs by size, play style, and energy level.** This is the most consistently recommended practice and measurably reduces conflict and stress.
- Ensure **unobstructed sightlines** so staff can supervise the whole group.
- Provide **quiet rest/decompression areas** — even social dogs need breaks; overstimulation is a leading cause of scuffles.

## Vaccination requirements

Require **proof of core vaccinations before any dog enters group care**:

- **Rabies** (legally required in most states)
- **Distemper / Parvovirus / Adenovirus (hepatitis)** — usually the combined **DHPP/DAPP**
- **Bordetella** (kennel cough) — standard for congregate settings; many require it every 6–12 months
- **Canine influenza (CIV/H3N2/H3N8)** — increasingly required, region-dependent

Puppies should **not enter general daycare until the core series is complete (~16 weeks)** because their immune systems are still developing and group settings carry real parvovirus risk. Track expiration dates and **block check-in on expired records** (software should flag this automatically).

## Intake & evaluation

- **Screen before admission:** verify vaccines, collect a behavior/medical history, emergency contact, vet on file, feeding/medication instructions.
- Run a **temperament/behavior evaluation** before the first group play; assign to an appropriate group.
- **Re-evaluate periodically** — dogs change with age and experience; reassessment lets you adjust groupings before problems arise.

## Intake forms should capture

- Owner + emergency contact + authorized pickup
- Vet name/phone + medical conditions, allergies, medications
- Vaccination records (with dates)
- Feeding schedule and food
- Behavior notes (resource guarding, fear triggers, dog/people reactivity)
- Spay/neuter status
- Signed liability waiver and authorization for emergency vet care (with a cost cap)

## Incident protocol

When something happens, **documentation + transparent client communication are critical**:

1. **Separate** dogs using trained techniques (never reach into a fight bare-handed; use barriers/air horns/water as trained).
2. **Assess injuries** and provide first aid; transport to vet if warranted.
3. **Contact the owner promptly** with what happened and next steps.
4. **File a written incident report** — date/time, dogs involved, staff present, sequence of events, injuries, action taken, follow-up. Treat reports as **improvement and accountability tools**, not just paperwork.
5. Review patterns periodically to adjust grouping, staffing, or admission decisions.

## Staffing & training

- Train all floor staff to **read canine stress/body language** and intervene **before** escalation.
- Maintain pet first-aid/CPR certification on staff.
- Document standard operating procedures (feeding, cleaning/sanitation, fire/evacuation, lost-dog, medical emergency).
