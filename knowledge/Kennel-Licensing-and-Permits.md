## 1. State License — Illinois Animal Welfare Act (the binding requirement)

Illinois regulates kennels at the **state** level through the **Illinois Department of Agriculture, Bureau of Animal Health and Welfare**, under the **Illinois Animal Welfare Act** and the Animal Welfare Rules (8 Ill. Admin. Code Part 25).

**Who must be licensed:** The Bureau licenses pet shop operators, dog dealers, **kennel operators**, cat and dog breeders, animal control facilities, animal shelters, **day care operators**, guard dog services, and horse rescues. A dog-boarding-and-training operation like Happy Pup Manor needs the **kennel operator** license (and should confirm whether a separate **day care operator** license is required if daycare is sold as a distinct service — a separate application is required per license type).

**Fee:** **$350 application fee per license type** (non-refundable). A separate application + separate fee for each license category you operate under.

**Term / renewal:** Licenses **expire annually on June 30**. Renew before that date through the Department's online portal (requires license number + PIN).

**Inspection:** The Department conducts a **preliminary inspection before issuing** the license, and you must reach compliance **within 60 days** of the Department receiving your application to retain the fee. Expect periodic re-inspection thereafter (the Act authorizes inspection of licensed facilities; exact recurring cadence is set by the Bureau — confirm current schedule with them).

**Boarding-specific operating rules (8 Ill. Admin. Code § 25.120):**
- **No dog or cat may be accepted for boarding or training unless vaccinated for distemper**, with proof furnished to the operator. (Rabies vaccination is also standard; confirm current required vaccine list with the Bureau.)
- **Records must be kept for 12 months** after each boarding/training, stating: owner name, address, phone; expected duration of stay; service to be provided; and the owner's agent for emergency contact.

**How / where to apply:** Submit the fillable Initial License Application with payment (**check or money order only**) to:
Illinois Department of Agriculture, Bureau of Animal Health and Welfare, 801 E. Sangamon Ave, Springfield, IL 62702-1813.
Application + rules: https://agr.illinois.gov/animals/animalhealth/applying-for-a-license.html and https://agr.illinois.gov/animals/animalhealth/animal-welfare.html

## 2. USDA / Federal (APHIS, Animal Welfare Act) — generally NOT applicable

**Pet boarding kennels are EXEMPT from USDA/APHIS federal AWA licensing.** Boarding/caring for animals owned by others is not a regulated activity requiring a federal license.

**The one exception to watch:** If Happy Pup Manor ever **receives or ships regulated animals via a public/commercial carrier without the owner present** (acting as an "intermediate handler"), that activity is federally regulated and would require APHIS **registration as an intermediate handler**. Standard "owner drops the dog off, owner picks it up" boarding does **not** trigger this. (If the business adds animal-transport/ground-shipping as a service, re-check this.)
Source: APHIS licensing guidance — https://www.aphis.usda.gov/awa/apply and the AWA regulations.

## 3. Local Zoning / Conditional-Use Permit (Lake County or Village of Grayslake)

In Illinois, **zoning is entirely local** — the state does not say which zones must allow kennels. Each municipality or county decides. So the state license above is necessary but **not sufficient**; the parcel must also be zoned to allow a commercial kennel, or hold a conditional-use permit / special-use permit.

**Village of Grayslake definition (if the parcel is inside village limits):** A "kennel" is any place where any number of dogs are kept in connection with boarding, care, or breeding for a fee; OR any place keeping **more than four (4) dogs over four (4) months old** for any purpose. A commercial kennel almost certainly is NOT a by-right use in a residential/agricultural district and will require a **conditional use permit (special use)** — granted only after a **public hearing and zoning-board approval**.
Ordinance: https://www.villageofgrayslake.com/DocumentCenter/View/13667/Chapter-17---Zoning-Ordinance---September-2023

**If the parcel is unincorporated Lake County** (likely, given 30 acres on W Chardon Rd — **verify**): the **Lake County Planning, Building & Development department** governs zoning and the conditional/special-use process, not the Village. The 30-acre size helps — large-acreage agricultural/estate parcels are the most common place a kennel special-use is granted.

**Action item:** Pull the parcel in the Lake County GIS/parcel viewer to confirm (a) jurisdiction (county vs. village), (b) current zoning district, and (c) whether a kennel is permitted, conditional, or prohibited there. Then apply to the correct body for the special/conditional use permit if needed. Contact Village of Grayslake Planning OR Lake County Planning, Building & Development.

## 4. Insurance (not a government license, but practically required)

State licensing does not mandate a specific policy, but a boarding/training kennel should carry, at minimum:
- **General liability** (property damage, bodily injury to clients on-site).
- **Animal bailee / care, custody & control (CCC)** coverage — covers injury, illness, escape, or death of an animal in your care, which standard general liability typically **excludes**.
- **Professional liability** for the training side.
- **Commercial property** for the facility, and **workers' compensation** (required in Illinois once you have employees).
Confirm exact requirements with an Illinois commercial insurance broker; "kennel/pet-care business" policies are a standard product. (Flagged: specific limits/required lines are business-judgment, not a verified statutory mandate.)

## 5. Employee / Handler Rules

- **Illinois workers' compensation** is mandatory for businesses with employees.
- The Animal Welfare Act/Rules set **animal-care standards** (housing, sanitation, feeding, vet care) that staff must meet; staffing-ratio specifics are not fixed in statute — confirm current rules with the Bureau.
- Standard employment-law obligations apply (Illinois minimum wage, I-9, etc.) — outside the scope of animal licensing but part of opening.

5. ☐ Bind insurance incl. animal bailee/CCC + workers' comp.
6. ☐ Calendar the **June 30 annual renewal**.
7. ☐ Re-check USDA intermediate-handler registration ONLY if you add owner-absent animal transport.
