# Local Marketing & Customer Acquisition for a Suburban Boarding / Daycare / Grooming Business

Practical playbook for Happy Pup Manor (Grayslake, IL — north Lake County). The Grayslake / Lake County market is competitive: ~22 dog boarding kennels serve the 60030 area, and named local competitors include Central Bark Grayslake, Shag Bark Pet Retreat & Spa (Gurnee/Round Lake), and Bark University. Differentiation and a steady acquisition engine matter more here than in a thin market.

This doc covers the channels that actually move the needle for a facility-based boarding/daycare/grooming business, in rough priority order, plus a monthly marketing calendar.

---

## 1. Referral partnerships (highest-ROI channel)

Most boarding/daycare customers arrive via a vet referral or word-of-mouth, not a cold ad. Build a referral network of the businesses that already have a trust relationship with local dog owners.

**Who to partner with (Grayslake / north Lake County):**
- Veterinary clinics in Grayslake, Gurnee, Round Lake, Libertyville, Mundelein, Lake Villa
- Independent groomers who don't board (and can refer boarding/daycare)
- Pet supply stores and feed stores
- Dog trainers, dog parks, and breeders
- Real estate agents (new-mover welcome packets) and apartment/HOA communities that allow dogs

**How to structure it:**
- Offer the partner's clients an exclusive new-client discount (e.g. "$20 off the first boarding stay" or "free daycare assessment day").
- Reciprocate: refer your clients to that vet/groomer/trainer so the relationship is two-way, not extractive.
- Leave a small stack of cards or a QR-coded flyer at their front desk. Refresh it monthly so it never goes stale.
- Track which partner each new client names ("How did you hear about us?") so you know which relationships to invest in.

**Outreach tactic:** A simple cold-email or in-person walk-in to ~20 local pet businesses proposing a cross-referral arrangement is a proven low-cost acquisition move. Lead with what's in it for them (a discount for their clients), not what you want.

## 2. Google Business Profile + local search ("dog boarding near me")

When a Grayslake dog owner needs boarding, they Google it. You need to own that moment.

**Foundation (free, do this first):**
- Claim and fully complete your **Google Business Profile** — correct hours, service area (Grayslake + neighboring towns), photos of the actual facility and real dogs, services listed (boarding, daycare, grooming).
- Keep NAP (name, address, phone) identical across Google, Yelp, Bing, Apple Maps, Care.com, Rover, and Boarding.com listings. Inconsistent info hurts local ranking.
- Post photo updates regularly — Google rewards active profiles.

**Google Local Services Ads (LSA):**
- LSA places you at the very top of search with a "Google Screened/Guaranteed" badge and is **pay-per-lead** (you pay for a call/message, not a click), which suits a local service business well.
- *Caveat (flag): availability of the Local Services Ads category for pet boarding/daycare varies by market and changes over time — verify the category is currently offered for Grayslake before budgeting for it. If LSA isn't available, fall back to standard Google Search Ads.*

**Google Search Ads (always available):**
- Target high-intent local keywords: "dog boarding near me," "dog daycare Grayslake," "dog boarding Lake County," "pet grooming Grayslake," "dog boarding Gurnee."
- Geo-fence tightly to your real drive radius (Grayslake, Gurnee, Round Lake, Libertyville, Mundelein, Lake Villa, Wadsworth) — don't pay for clicks from Chicago proper.
- Point ads at a dedicated landing page with a phone number, online booking, and reviews — not your homepage.

## 3. Nextdoor + local Facebook groups

Hyperlocal social is where suburban neighbors ask "who do you use for boarding?" — and ~63% of Nextdoor members have a pet in the household, with 100k+ pet-related posts monthly platform-wide.

**Nextdoor:**
- Create a free Business Page; upload real, high-quality photos of your facility and dogs.
- Run **Nextdoor Local Deals / Sponsored posts** targeted to Grayslake and adjacent neighborhoods.
- Encourage happy clients to recommend you — neighbor recommendations are the platform's strongest signal.

**Local Facebook groups:**
- Join Grayslake / Lake County / north-suburbs community and pet groups. Be a helpful presence (answer dog questions), not a spammer — most groups limit overt self-promotion.
- Post seasonal reminders ("Holiday boarding is filling — reserve now") where group rules allow.
- A business Facebook page with regular "dog of the day" / report-card photos doubles as social proof.

## 4. Review-generation system (the compounding asset)

Reviews are the single biggest trust signal for a local pet business and directly affect Google ranking. Treat review generation as a **system**, not an occasional ask.

**Make it automatic:**
- Use kennel software (Gingr, PetExec, Kennel Connection, or a tool like Broadly) to auto-send a review request by **SMS and email shortly after each stay or grooming visit**, with a one-tap link straight to your Google review page. Timing matters — sending right after a happy pickup gets the highest response.
- Place **QR-code review stands** at the front desk / pickup counter linking directly to Google reviews so clients can leave one from their phone before they leave.

**Spread across platforms:** Prioritize Google (ranking + visibility), then Yelp, then Facebook. Don't funnel everyone to one site.

**Respond to every review** — thank the positive ones, address the negative ones calmly and publicly. Prospective clients read your responses, not just the stars.

**Volume target:** aim for a steady trickle (a few new reviews per week) rather than a one-time burst, which looks unnatural and fades in ranking value.

## 5. New-client offers

A first-visit offer lowers the risk of trying you over an incumbent competitor.

**Offers that work for this business:**
- **Free daycare assessment / evaluation day** — standard in the industry, doubles as a temperament screen and gets the dog (and owner) in the door.
- **First-stay discount** — e.g. "$25 off your first boarding reservation" or "first day of daycare free."
- **New-puppy / new-mover welcome package** — partner with vets and real-estate agents to distribute.
- **Bundle intro** — "Try a grooming + daycare day" to cross-expose your services.

Keep the offer tied to capturing contact info and booking the *next* visit (see retention doc) — acquisition is wasted if it's a one-and-done.

## 6. Seasonal demand spikes — and how to pre-book them

Boarding demand is highly seasonal. Capacity is finite, so the goal is to **fill peak periods early at full (or premium) price** and smooth the troughs.

**Peak periods and how far ahead clients book:**
- **Thanksgiving** — fills ~4–6 weeks out
- **Christmas / New Year's** — fills ~8–10 weeks out (book earliest of all)
- **Summer vacation (Jun–Aug)** — book ~6–8 weeks ahead
- **Memorial Day, July 4th, Labor Day, Spring Break** — long weekends spike sharply

**Industry norm:** facilities commonly apply a **20–50% holiday surcharge** and **require a deposit to hold peak reservations**. Both are standard and accepted — deposits also cut no-shows when availability is scarce. (Aligns with the reseller-side principle: charging a premium at peak demand is a legitimate business choice, not something to apologize for.)

**Pre-booking playbook:**
1. **Open holiday booking early and announce it.** Email/text your existing client list 8–10 weeks before each peak: "Holiday boarding is now open — reserve before we fill."
2. **Require a deposit** to lock the reservation (and protect against no-shows).
3. **Waitlist** once full — captures overflow demand and signals scarcity that drives earlier booking next time.
4. **Reward early bookers** (e.g. small discount or priority slot for reserving 6+ weeks out) to pull demand forward and de-risk your calendar.
5. **Fill the troughs** (Jan–Feb, post-summer) with daycare promos, grooming packages, and membership pushes (see retention doc).

## 7. Simple monthly marketing calendar

A repeatable rhythm so nothing depends on remembering. Lake County seasonality is built in.

| Month | Primary push | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Jan** | Win-back lapsed clients; daycare membership push | Slow season — fill with packages |
| **Feb** | Grooming promo (winter coats); referral drive | "Valentine's pampering" angle |
| **Mar** | **Open summer + spring-break boarding**; refresh vet/groomer flyers | Spring break travel |
| **Apr** | New-client offers; ramp Google/Nextdoor ads pre-summer | Lighter-coat grooming season |
| **May** | **Memorial Day boarding deposits**; summer membership drive | First big summer weekend |
| **Jun** | Summer boarding in full swing; daycare for restless kids-home dogs | Peak begins |
| **Jul** | **July 4th boarding** (book by mid-June); review-generation push | Noise-anxiety boarding angle |
| **Aug** | Labor Day boarding; back-to-school daycare ("dog's alone all day") | Daycare cross-sell |
| **Sep** | **Open Thanksgiving booking** (~6 wks out); fall grooming | Pull demand forward |
| **Oct** | Thanksgiving deposits closing; **open Christmas/NYE booking** | Earliest holiday push |
| **Nov** | Christmas/NYE filling; holiday surcharge live; waitlist | Highest-revenue window |
| **Dec** | NYE last slots; collect reviews from holiday clients; plan Q1 win-back | Capture social proof |

**Every-month constants:**
- Auto review requests firing after each stay/groom.
- Fresh Google Business Profile + Nextdoor + Facebook photo posts (report cards, "dog of the day").
- Refill partner-business flyer stacks.
- Check "how did you hear about us?" data and shift spend to the best-performing channel.
